What the two colours are

Every point is at some distance from the line. Drop a perpendicular onto it: the foot of that perpendicular is where the point lands after projection, and the segment you dropped is everything the projection discards. Squaring and averaging each of those gives the two numbers in the bar.

The kept quantity is the variance of the projected coordinates — the spread of the dots along the line. The lost quantity is the mean squared length of the residual segments. Rotating the line trades one against the other, and their sum does not move:

variance along u + mean squared residual = tr(S) = λ1 + λ2

This is why the two textbook definitions of PCA describe the same algorithm. Choosing the direction of maximum projected variance and choosing the direction of minimum reconstruction error are not two criteria that happen to agree — with a fixed total, they are one criterion stated twice.

What the curve is

The lower plot is the kept variance as a function of the angle, J(θ) = u(θ)⊤ S u(θ). Sweeping θ from 0° to 180° covers every direction once, so a point on that curve is a direction. Its highest point is the first principal component and its lowest is the second, and they sit exactly 90° apart — which is the whole of the eigenvector result, before any algebra.

The curve is always a sinusoid in 2θ. Its mean height is half the total spread and its amplitude is (λ1 − λ2)/2, so a flat curve means the two eigenvalues are equal and no direction is preferred.

Things to try

  • Set the correlation to zero with unequal spread. The best direction snaps to a coordinate axis: uncorrelated features are already principal components.
  • Flatten the curve completely with No preferred direction, then rotate. Nothing changes. Every direction is equally good, the eigenvectors are not unique, and any loading you reported would be an artefact of your library's sort order.
  • Add one bad point and drag it far out. The line follows it. Variance is a squared quantity, so a single outlier can pull the first component towards itself — and the fit looks no worse from the numbers alone.
  • Change the units of x2 and watch the best direction rotate. Nothing about the data changed except a multiplication, which is why PCA on features with mixed units usually means standardising first.
  • Turn centring off. The line abandons the shape of the cloud and points at the mean instead, because the matrix now measures distance from the origin rather than spread about the centre.
  • Drop the sample size to 20 and redraw a few times. Watch how far the best direction wanders when the cloud is nearly round.

What this leaves out

Two dimensions hide the step that causes most implementation errors: sorting. With more than two components the eigenvalues come out of a decomposition in no particular order, and the second principal direction is whichever one has the second largest eigenvalue — not whichever appears second. Everything else on this page generalises unchanged to any number of dimensions.
