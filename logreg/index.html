What you are looking at

The model is p̂ = σ(w₁x₁ + w₂x₂ + w₀). Its boundary is where the linear score is zero, w₁x₁ + w₂x₂ + w₀ = 0, which is a straight line. Every other probability level is also a line where the score is constant, so the dashed lines at p̂ = 0.1 and 0.9 run parallel to the boundary. Point the mouse anywhere on the plot to read p̂ there. The weight vector w = (w₁, w₂) points across the bands, toward class 1, and its length sets how quickly p̂ goes from 0.5 to near 0 or 1.

The red halo on each point is its pull on the next gradient step, |p̂ − y|.

The right panel looks at the same model side-on. Measured along w, a point at distance d from the boundary has p̂ = σ(‖w‖ d), so the two-feature model is the familiar one-dimensional S-curve laid across the plane. The bands on the left are slices of this one curve, and ‖w‖ sets how steep it is.

The take-home message

The boundary is decided by all the points together. Points on the wrong side or close to the line pull hard; points deep on their own side pull very little, but never exactly nothing.

A limitation

When the two classes can be separated by a line, logistic regression has no single answer. Every separating line can push the loss toward zero just by growing ‖w‖, and once every point is on its correct side, all the pulls are close to zero. Nothing then pushes the line toward the middle of the gap, so where it ends up depends on where training started and when it stopped. The margin readout, the distance from the line to the nearest point, makes the difference visible.

Things to try

  • The page opens at w = 0, where every point has p̂ = 0.5 and an equal halo. Click One step a few times and watch the line appear and turn as the halos shrink. Train from scratch returns to w = 0.
  • Drag a point deep into its own class’s territory. Its halo and its stick almost vanish, and the line barely moves. Drag it across the line and it pulls the whole line toward it.
  • Flip the label of a point far from the boundary. One confident mistake shifts and tilts the line, and the bands spread out.
  • Raise the gap until the classes separate and the margin readout shows a number after training. Note the margin, then pause, draw a starting line that separates the classes but runs close to one of them, and run. The line stays near where you drew it, with a much smaller margin. Press Train from scratch and run again: the same data, a different line.
  • On separable data, also watch the bands: they keep squeezing toward the boundary, ‖w‖ keeps growing and the S-curve on the right steepens toward a step: with nothing left to get wrong, the model only grows more confident.
