What you are looking at

The network has two inputs, two hidden neurons with the activation g chosen above, and one sigmoid output neuron trained with the cross-entropy loss. Hidden neuron j computes zj = wj·x + bj and hj = g(zj); the output computes s = v1h1 + v2h2 + c and p̂ = σ(s). Blue edges are positive weights, red negative; thicker means larger. Forward values appear in the nodes, errors δ in orange above them, and gradients in orange on the edges, next to the neuron they feed.

The default weights and sample are the worked example of the PRML notes (Section 3.3), so every number can be checked against the hand calculation.

The take-home message

Backpropagation hands each neuron one number, its δ. For the output neuron it is p̂ − y. Each hidden neuron receives that error weighted by its outgoing weight vj and gated by the slope g′(zj) of its activation. Every weight then moves by η times δ times the input it carries.

Things to try

  • With the defaults, step through all eight stages. At the hidden errors, the second neuron is off (z2 < 0), so its ReLU gate closes and neither of its weights moves.
  • Choose the sample (0, 1). Both hidden neurons are on now, yet the first weight of each gets a zero gradient: the input it carries, x1, is 0.
  • Switch the activation to sigmoid. The gate never closes completely, but its slope is at most 0.25, so every hidden δ is smaller than the output’s.
  • Press Random weights a few times with sigmoid. When a hidden score is large, the slope is nearly zero and that neuron barely learns: saturation.
  • Take a step on (1, 0), then switch to (0, 0) and take another. Each step lowers the loss on its own sample; whether it helps the other samples is not guaranteed.
