Interactive Teaching Demos

Short, hands-on demos for machine learning that run entirely in the browser — nothing to install, nothing uploaded, nothing stored.

Logistic Regression

Logistic regressionSigmoidGradient descent

Where the Line Falls

Train logistic regression step by step and watch every point pull the decision boundary in proportion to its error. A side view along w shows the sigmoid behind the probabilities, and on separable data the final line depends on where training started.

Classifier Evaluation

ROC curvePrecision–recallAUC

Where the Threshold Goes

Move a classifier's decision threshold and watch the confusion matrix, ROC curve and precision–recall curve respond. Shows why ROC-AUC and PR-AUC disagree when positives are rare, and what neither can see: calibration.

Principal Component Analysis (PCA)

PCAVarianceReconstruction error

Where the Principal Direction Goes

Rotate a line through a cloud of points and watch its spread split into what the projection keeps and what it throws away. The best direction keeps the most variance and loses the least: the first principal component.

PCAEigenvaluesDimensionality reduction

How Many Components Is Enough?

Rebuild handwritten-digit images from a few principal components, see what survives and where the missing part goes, and use the eigenvalue spectrum to decide how many components to keep.

Support Vector Machines (SVM)

SVMMarginSupport vectors

Why This Line?

Try to separate two classes with the widest possible street, then let the SVM find the maximum margin. Drag points to see that only the support vectors move the boundary, and turn C to trade margin width against violations (soft margin).

Open demo →
SVMKernelsOverfitting

Bumps and Boundaries

Fit SVMs with linear, polynomial and Gaussian (RBF) kernels to curved data, and sweep C and γ from too smooth to too wiggly while comparing training and test accuracy. A 3-D view shows the kernel trick: a curved boundary becoming a flat plane.

Open demo →

Neural Networks

Neural networksHidden layerBackpropagation

The Hidden Layer Bends Space

Train a small neural network and follow the same points in two views: the input space, where the boundary curves, and the hidden-layer space, where it is a straight line. A live diagram of the network shows its weights and the values each neuron computes for any point.
